# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

OctoAcme follows a lightweight but structured project lifecycle that starts in initiation, where teams define the problem, SMART goals, success metrics, stakeholders, risks, and a go/no-go decision to enter planning. In planning, teams convert approved initiatives into a prioritized and estimated backlog, document dependencies, align milestones and release timelines, and confirm a shared Definition of Done with an initial QA approach.

Execution and tracking focus on predictable delivery through recurring team rhythms, visible board states, and disciplined pull request practices. Teams commonly use board stages such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done, and pair that flow with regular standups, weekly syncs, and sprint or milestone demos to surface progress, blockers, and dependency risks early. Pull requests are expected to stay small and reviewable, link back to issues and acceptance criteria, and pass CI checks (tests, linting, and security scanning) before approval and merge.

Roles and ownership are clearly defined across Project Managers, Product Managers, Developers, QA/testing contributors, and stakeholders. Project Managers coordinate timelines, risks, and communications; Product Managers own outcomes and prioritization; Developers implement and validate solutions; QA supports acceptance validation; and stakeholders provide input and approvals that keep delivery aligned to business value.

Communication and quality are continuous practices across the lifecycle, not final-stage steps. OctoAcme maintains risk registers, stakeholder-specific updates, and clear escalation paths from team triage through sponsor-level escalation, while reinforcing quality with unit, integration, and smoke testing, CI checks (including security scanning), release readiness criteria, rollback planning, and post-release verification. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, and incidents convert lessons learned into actionable improvements.
